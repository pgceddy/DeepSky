Privacy Policy

Last updated September 2026

In short: Deep Sky Scanner does its work on your device. Your location and the direction you're pointing your phone never leave it. The app has no account, no analytics, and no advertising.

What the app uses, and why

Deep Sky Scanner needs two pieces of information from your device to work at all:

  • Location. Used to calculate which stars, galaxies, and nebulae are above your horizon right now. This calculation happens entirely on your device.
  • Compass and motion sensors. Used to determine which direction and angle you're pointing your phone or tablet, so the app can tell you what's in front of you.

Neither of these is transmitted anywhere. There's no server that receives your location or orientation, because the app doesn't have one for this purpose.

What does leave your device

The app fetches deep-sky object data (names, coordinates, magnitudes) from a public astronomical catalog (GAVO's OpenNGC service) so it can show you what's nearby. This is a one-way, anonymous request for catalog data — it does not include your location, your device identity, or anything else about you. If you're offline, the app falls back to a catalog copy stored on your device.

What the app does not do

  • No account or sign-in
  • No analytics or crash reporting that identifies you
  • No advertising or ad tracking
  • No selling or sharing of data with third parties

Data storage

Any preferences or logged observations you save in the app stay in local storage on your device. Uninstalling the app removes them.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the "last updated" date at the top of this page will change with it. Continued use of the app after an update means you accept the revised policy.
